What is Research Source Organizer?

Research Source Organizer is a browser-based tool for students writing research papers. It helps you collect, annotate, and organize your sources — and then use them to build a bibliography and draft a paper outline.

Everything runs locally in your browser. No account, no login, and no data is sent to any server. Your sources are saved automatically in your browser's local storage between sessions.

You can also upload PDF or DOCX files to extract their text directly into the source form — useful for pulling quotes and passages from reading you have already done.

How to Use It
1 — Add Source
Enter the title, author, year, and publication. Write a 2–3 sentence annotation summarizing the source and how it connects to your topic. Assign a stance and topic tags.
2 — Browse & Filter
Search your source library by keyword, stance, type, or tag. Edit or delete any entry. The stats strip shows your stance balance at a glance.
3 — Theme Map
See all sources grouped by topic tag with color-coded stance borders. Use this to spot gaps — topics where you have no counter-arguments, or themes with only one source.
4 — Bibliography
Generate a formatted annotated bibliography in MLA 9th, APA 7th, or Chicago style. Copy individual citations or download the full bibliography as a text file.
5 — Outline Helper
Type your thesis statement and see your sources automatically sorted into paper sections by stance — Background, Supporting Evidence, Counter-Arguments, and Neutral Perspectives.
Understanding Stance

Every source you add is assigned one of four stances relative to your thesis. This drives the Theme Map, the Outline Helper, and the stats strip in Browse.

Supports The source provides evidence, data, or arguments that directly back your thesis. These are your primary building blocks.
Challenges The source argues against your thesis or presents evidence that complicates it. Including these makes your paper more credible — acknowledge and rebut them.
Background The source provides context, definitions, history, or statistics that frame your topic without taking a side. Usually appears in your introduction.
Neutral The source presents nuance, multiple perspectives, or findings that don't fit neatly into support or challenge. Useful for a balanced conclusion.
Citation Formats

The Bibliography tab generates formatted citations automatically. The tool applies standard rules for each format — but always verify citations against your style guide before submitting.

MLA 9th Edition
Common in humanities, literature, and language courses. Author's last name first. Article titles in quotation marks; book and journal titles italicized.
Smith, Jane. "Article Title." Journal Name, 2023.
APA 7th Edition
Standard in social sciences, psychology, and education. Date appears immediately after author name. Only the first word of a title is capitalized.
Smith, J. (2023). Title of work. Publisher.
Chicago
Widely used in history, fine arts, and some humanities disciplines. Similar structure to MLA but with variation in punctuation and article/book treatment.
Smith, Jane. "Article Title." Journal Name (2023).
